This document contains materials associated with \rustdoc{accuracy/fn}{accuracy\_to\_discrete\_gaussian\_scale}.

By \rustdoc{accuracy/fn}{discrete\_gaussian\_scale\_to\_accuracy},
for positive integer $a$ and positive scale $s$, the probability of error less than $a$ is:

\begin{align*}
    P[Y<a] = \frac{\sum_{y=0}^{a - 1} (1 + 1[y \ne 0]) e^{-(y/s)^2/2}}{\sum_{z \in \mathbb{Z}} e^{-(z/s)^2/2}}
\end{align*}

We use binary search to approximate the crossing at target tail probability $\alpha\in(0,1)$.
A loose upper bound is provided by \rustdoc{accuracy/fn}{accuracy\_to\_gaussian\_scale}.
The binary search approximates the crossing from above, where
\begin{align*}
    \alpha \le 1 - \frac{\sum_{y=0}^{a - 1} (1 + 1[y \ne 0]) e^{-(y/s)^2/2}}{\sum_{z \in \mathbb{Z}} e^{-(z/s)^2/2}}
\end{align*}

The target tail is at most $\alpha$ on the lower-scale side. The implementation truncates nonnegative real accuracy to an integer in the sum; the displayed formula applies to positive integer thresholds. Zero scale is the degenerate noise distribution, not a case of the positive-scale formula. The search is numerical, not a directed-rounding guarantee.

Proves soundness of \rustdoc{measurements/fn}{make\_canonical\_noise}.
The constructor compiles finitely many approximate-DP bounds from a
\texttt{PrivacyGuarantee}, rather than trusting a numerical tradeoff callback.

\section{Hoare Triple}
\subsection*{Precondition}
The input domain is \texttt{AtomDomain<f64>} without NaN, the metric is
\texttt{AbsoluteDistance<f64>}, and sensitivity \texttt{d\_in} is finite and
nonnegative. Invalid parameters are rejected. Compilation evaluates the
specification at an ordered finite epsilon grid, including an accessible
approximate-DP certificate endpoint. Sampled epsilon values must be finite and
nonnegative and deltas must lie in $[0,1]$.
No additional caller assertion of symmetry, convexity, or monotonicity of the
sampled numerical bounds is required.

\section{Certified compilation}
Let $(\epsilon_i,\delta_i)$ be the exact sampled floating-point values,
interpreted as real numbers. Directed interval exponentiation followed by a
lower-directed narrowing supplies a rational $q_i$ with
$1\leq q_i\leq \exp(\epsilon_i)$. Unsupported numerical ranges and provider
errors are rejected, not replaced by weaker bounds. Define $a_i=1-\delta_i$
using exact rational arithmetic and
\[
 f_i(\alpha)=\max\left(0,\ a_i-q_i\alpha,\
                         \frac{a_i-\alpha}{q_i}\right).
\]
Both slopes use the same rational $q_i$, with its exact reciprocal.
Thus $f_i$ is the symmetric approximate-DP tradeoff for parameters
$(\log(q_i),\delta_i)$: its epigraph in the unit square is described by
\[
 \beta\geq0,\qquad \beta+q_i\alpha\geq a_i,\qquad
 \alpha+q_i\beta\geq a_i.
\]
These inequalities are invariant under exchanging $\alpha$ and $\beta$.
Each $f_i$ is convex, nonincreasing and lies below $1-\alpha$.

A tradeoff function gives the minimum type-II error achievable by a
randomized test at a given bound on its type-I error.
The compiled envelope $f=\max_i f_i$ is convex, continuous, nonincreasing,
and satisfies $0\leq f(\alpha)\leq 1-\alpha$.
These properties characterize tradeoff functions
\cite[Section~2]{awan2023canonical} (citing Dong et al., Proposition~2.2).
Its epigraph is the intersection of the symmetric epigraphs above, so
$f(\alpha)\leq\beta$ if and only if $f(\beta)\leq\alpha$ for
$\alpha,\beta\in[0,1]$. This is symmetry in the generalized-inverse sense,
including any zero plateau.

The fixed point of $f_i$ is $c_i=a_i/(1+q_i)$. Put $c=\max_i c_i$.
Since every $f_i$ is nonincreasing, $f_i(c)\leq c$, and a component attaining
this maximum has $f_i(c)=c$. Therefore $f(c)=c$ exactly, and nonincrease
of $f$ implies uniqueness of the fixed point.
Compilation requires $0<c<1/2$, excluding the identically zero and
perfect-privacy tradeoffs. In particular, $f(1/2)\leq c<1/2$,
so $f$ is nontrivial as required by the canonical noise theorem.

\section{Sampling and privacy}
Let $F_f$ be the canonical CDF in \cite[Definition~3.7]{awan2023canonical},
and let $N$ have this CDF. Write $g(x)$ for clamping the input to the
finite representable endpoints and $\Delta=\texttt{d\_in}$.
Since NaN is excluded, converting $g(x)$ to a rational is exact and
infallible, including when $x$ is infinite before clamping.
By the postcondition of
\rustdoc{traits/samplers/psrn/canonical/struct}{CanonicalRV}'s inverse CDF,
and the sampling contract of
\rustdoc{traits/samplers/psrn/struct}{PartialSample}\texttt{.value},
the function returns the nearest floating-point representation of
$g(x)+\Delta N$.
The compiled rational tradeoff cannot return an evaluation error;
specification certification errors occur at construction.
As in the sampler contract, random-generator errors are treated as
data-independent.

For $\Delta>0$, clamping is nonexpansive, including at infinite endpoints
(with equal infinities at distance zero). Thus for any inputs at distance
at most $\Delta$, their clamped values differ by at most $\Delta$.
By \cite[Theorem~3.9 and Definition~3.1]{awan2023canonical},
adding $\Delta N$ to such values satisfies $f$-DP; rounding to a float
is postprocessing. If $\Delta=0$, only equal inputs are admissible and
the ideal output is the deterministic value $g(x)$.
More generally, at distance zero the clamped values and output laws
coincide for any $\Delta$, giving perfect privacy.

The upper envelope is represented by affine segments with increasing
slopes and increasing starting abscissas. To construct it, sort the lines by
slope, retaining only the highest intercept for each slope. When a new line
is considered, it dominates the previous line to the right of their
intersection. If the intersection precedes or equals the previous segment's
start, that segment can never be active and is removed. Otherwise the
intersection is the new segment's start. Lines starting beyond one are
irrelevant on $[0,1]$. Induction gives the exact upper envelope on that
interval; binary search by segment start evaluates it exactly.

Since the mechanism satisfies $f$-DP, its privacy profile is bounded by
\[
    h_f(\epsilon)=\max_{0\leq\alpha\leq1}
       \left(1-f(\alpha)-e^\epsilon\alpha\right).
\]
The function being maximized is concave and piecewise affine.
For any rational $q\leq e^\epsilon$, its corresponding objective is an
upper bound and has slope $-s-q$ on a segment of slope $s$.
The first segment with slope at least $-q$ begins at a maximizing vertex.
Such a segment exists because the zero line is active at one.
Hence the computed value is a conservative upper bound.
In particular, at a sampled $\epsilon_i$, the compiled constraint implies
$h_f(\epsilon_i)\leq\delta_i$.

The value at $\alpha=0$ is $1-f(0)=\min_i\delta_i$, which is the eventual
constant value of the dual. All slope magnitudes are at most the largest
finite native float, so for $\epsilon\geq1024$ the maximum is attained at
zero. The profile stores this constant as exact additive slack.
The nonnegative remainder is computed rationally and its logarithm is
rounded upward using the software interval backend, without narrowing
the remainder to an ordinary native delta. When the remainder is zero it
returns negative infinity, preserving exact slack equality.

For positive distances no greater than \texttt{d\_in}, the privacy map returns
a fresh guarantee with only this profile. Zero distance returns a perfect
privacy log profile; it does not evaluate a rounded expression $1-\alpha$.
The descriptor advertises a profile, and advertises purity only when a zero
delta was sampled at a finite epsilon or the only admissible distance is zero.
No input RDP, zCDP or Gaussian-DP representation is copied to the output.
Consequently each successful privacy-map result certifies the sampled
mechanism for all admissible distances.

\section{Compilation}
For each sampled $(\epsilon_i,\delta_i)$, construct a rational
$a_i\in[0,e^{\epsilon_i}-1]$ by lower-directed \texttt{exp\_m1} and
narrowing, taking the maximum with $\epsilon_i$. For $\epsilon_i\geq1024$
use the largest finite float, which is less than $e^{1024}-1$.
Let $q_i=1+a_i$. Each constraint defines the symmetric tradeoff
\[
 f_i(u)=\max\{0,1-\delta_i-q_i u,(1-\delta_i-u)/q_i\}.
\]
The epigraph of $f=\max_i f_i$ is the intersection of the symmetric
epigraphs of its components. It is convex, continuous, nonincreasing,
lies in $[0,1-u]$, and satisfies $f(u)\leq v$ if and only if
$f(v)\leq u$. These are the tradeoff and generalized-inverse symmetry
conditions of \cite{awan2023canonical}.
Its fixed point is $c=\max_i(1-\delta_i)/(1+q_i)$, since at this point all
components are at most $c$ and at least one equals $c$. We require
$0<c<1/2$, excluding trivial and perfect-privacy tradeoffs.

For $u\leq c$, a component whose own fixed point is below $u$ is at
most $u$ and cannot exceed the envelope. Otherwise its steep branch
dominates its reciprocal branch. As $f(u)\geq u$, the envelope on
$[0,c]$ is therefore represented exactly by
\[
 D(u)=1-f(u)-u=\min_i(a_i u+\delta_i).
\]
To compile the lower envelope, order the lines by decreasing slope,
retaining the smallest intercept at each slope. A new line is below
the last line to the right of their intersection. If that intersection
is at or before the last line's start, the last line can be removed;
otherwise it is the new line's start. Lines starting at or beyond $c$
are irrelevant. Induction proves that the retained segments represent
$D$ on $[0,c]$.

\section{Quantile enclosures}
\lstinputlisting[language=Python,firstline=2]{./pseudocode/compiled_quantile.py}

Let $Q=F_f^{-1}$ be the canonical quantile of
\cite[Proposition~F.6]{awan2023canonical}. By symmetry it suffices to
evaluate the lower half, reversing the bound direction on reflection.
Write $T(u)=u+D(u)$. For $0<u<c$,
\[
 Q(u)=Q(T(u))-1,\qquad u<T(u)\leq1-c,
\]
and for $c\leq u\leq1-c$,
$Q(u)=(u-1/2)/(1-2c)$.
For a segment ending at $r\leq c$, the drift is $D(u)=au+\delta>0$.
If $a=0$, a translation
skips exactly $\lceil(r-u)/\delta\rceil$ steps to the next segment
or the central interval. Otherwise
\[
 T^n(u)=u+D(u)S_n,\qquad S_n=\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}(1+a)^j,\qquad
 S_{2n}=2S_n+aS_n^2.
\]
All terms are nonnegative. Starting at $S_1=1$, outward rounding
after each doubling gives lower and upper bounds on every binary sum.
If $u+D(u)\overline S_n<r$, all $n$ exact increasing iterates stay
on the same segment. Thus the block implements $Q(u)=Q(T^n(u))-n$.
Replacing the endpoint by the appropriate directed bound, optionally
rounded outward to the dyadic grid and capped at $r$, preserves
the quantile inequality by monotonicity of $Q$. Subtracting $n$ from
the offset accounts for the skipped steps. An exact single step
ensures progress if the surrogate remains in the segment.

For $0<u\leq c$, each line satisfies
$a_i+2\delta_i\geq(a_i+2)(1-2c)$ by the definition of $c$.
Consequently its drift is at least $2u(1-2c)$, and so is the minimum
drift of the compiled recurrence. This provides the scale for initial
grid precision and a strictly positive lower bound on drift over
the traversed interval. Each outer
iteration either advances by at least this minimum using an exact
step or reaches a new segment. The number of outer iterations and
binary powers is consequently bounded independently of precision.
The directed geometric-sum errors and grid-rounding errors tend
uniformly to zero on compact interior intervals as precision increases.
The quantile is continuous there: its central affine formula and
the finite recurrences agree at the segment and central boundaries.
The accumulated enclosure errors therefore converge to zero.
The PSRN contract requires converging certified bounds, not nested
bounds. Refinement increases grid precision while narrowing the uniform
interval, yielding the exact rounded sample almost surely.

\section{Sampling and privacy}
Let $g(x)$ clamp the input to the finite float endpoints. Conversion
to a rational is exact, and clamping is nonexpansive. By the preceding
enclosure argument and the \texttt{PartialSample.value} contract, the
function returns the nearest float to $g(x)+\Delta N$ where $N$ has
canonical CDF $F_f$. For $\Delta>0$, any pair of inputs at distance at
most $\Delta$ has clamped values at distance at most $\Delta$; the
canonical noise theorem \cite[Theorem~3.9]{awan2023canonical} gives
$f$-DP, preserved by rounding. For $\Delta=0$ the output is deterministic,
and at distance zero the two output laws always coincide. Entropy errors
are treated as data-independent by the sampler contract.

The hockey-stick bound is
\[
 h(\epsilon)=\max_{0\leq u\leq1}(1-f(u)-e^\epsilon u).
\]
On $[c,1]$ every component's reciprocal branch or zero is active
because $c$ is at least every component's fixed point. Hence the
envelope slopes lie in $[-1,0]$ and this objective is nonincreasing
there for $\epsilon\geq0$. It suffices to maximize $D(u)-a u$ over
$[0,c]$, using the same lower bound $a\leq e^\epsilon-1$.
The objective is concave and piecewise affine; the maximizing vertex
is the first segment whose slope is at most $a$, or $c$ if none exists.
Its value bounds $h(\epsilon)$ from above and is at most $\delta_i$
at every sampled $\epsilon_i$, by the defining constraint.
The value at zero, $D(0)=\min_i\delta_i$, is attained as the maximum
for $a$ at least the largest compiled slope. It is an original float
and is stored as exact profile slack. Subtract it rationally and round
the logarithm of the nonnegative remainder upward, returning negative
infinity for zero. The profile is conservative, including at exact
slack equality. The returned guarantee contains only this profile
at positive distances and a perfect-privacy profile at distance zero.
